# Athabasca unconformity uranium: what the record supports

This is the domain reference for the agent panel. Everything here is drawn from this project's research
reports 01 and 02, which cite their own sources. Where a claim is contested, both sides are given. Where a
claim is folklore, it is labelled and must never be used as support.

**The one rule that outranks everything below:** you may not state a number that did not come from a tool
result, with its value id. Not from this handbook, not from memory, not from arithmetic you did yourself. The
numbers written here are context for judgement, not values to quote.

---

## 1. The deposit model

Ore sits at or near the unconformity where flat-lying Athabasca Group sandstone rests on older deformed
basement. Two subtypes matter:

- **Unconformity-contact**: very high grade (IAEA gives 1–20% U).
- **Basement-hosted**: lower grade (0.10–0.50% U), and the unconformity may even be eroded away entirely, as
  at Kiggavik.

Deposits are small. Hurricane is 375 m along strike, up to 125 m wide and up to 12 m thick, at about 325 m
depth. **Every analysis cell in this project is 2 km across.** A cell is a search area, never a target.

Known depths span roughly 100–900 m: Larocque East's unconformity at 175 m, Hurricane ~325 m, Cigar Lake
480 m, McArthur River 530–640 m, Arrow 100–900 m.

### The organising element: a basement fault carrying graphite

A basement fault carrying graphitic metapelite gneiss makes the EM conductor. Fluids move along it, altering
and dissolving quartz in the sandstone above; the fault offsets the unconformity. Graphitic basement units are
"the source of most significant conductors in the Athabasca Basin, and are key empirical locators for U
deposits" (Jefferson et al. 2007).

Structural trap: at McArthur River "the unconformity offset where footwall faults... meet the P2 fault hosts
the ore pods". Corridor-scale control is real — Triple R lies on the Patterson Lake Conductive Corridor,
Hurricane tested a 15 km extension of the Larocque Lake graphitic conductor.

### Alteration, and why you cannot see it here

The clay alteration halo is the largest expression of the system: at Cigar Lake it reaches about 100 m below
the unconformity and about 300 m up into the sandstone. It is detectable by short-wave infrared, and kaolinite
crystallinity separates halo (Hinckley index 0.84–1.61) from bleached zones (0.45–0.7).

**None of that is in this project's data.** Those measurements live in company drill logs. Under 1–2 km of
sandstone, no satellite sees a halo. If alteration matters to your argument, it is an *unknown*, not an
absence, and the right move is to say what observation would settle it.

## 2. Signatures, and where they contradict each other

| Element | Response | Seen by |
|---|---|---|
| Graphitic gneiss in a basement fault | high conductivity | airborne and ground EM |
| Clay alteration, quartz dissolution | low resistivity, density, velocity | DC resistivity, gravity |
| Silicification | high resistivity, high density | gravity high |
| Unconformity offset at a fault | reflector step | seismic |
| Pelitic gneiss belts | magnetic low | aeromagnetics |
| Till, boulders, groundwater | K, eU, eTh, radon | radiometrics, radon |

**Three signatures are contested. A model that assumes a fixed sign is wrong.**

1. **Gravity.** "Gravity lows related to quartz dissolution" against McArthur River's "0.4 mGal amplitude
   gravity high attributed to silicification accompanying mineralization". The low west of Millennium is
   attributed partly to a fault and lake-bottom sediments, not to alteration over the deposit.
2. **Magnetics.** "Most Athabasca deposits correlate with magnetic lows" against deposits that "lack
   characteristic magnetic signatures", with P2 Main on "the southern margin of a moderate magnetic high".
3. **Radiometrics.** Reconnaissance airborne potassium missed an illite corridor that detailed ground
   spectrometry suggests it should show. The record does not resolve this scale dependence.

**None of these three datasets exists in this project at all** — no public magnetic, gravity or radiometric
grid is addressable by URL. Treat their absence as a gap in the evidence, not as a negative result.

### How the signature fails

- A conductor trace **plots up-dip of blind ore**, so proximity marks a corridor, not a position.
- Silicification can turn graphitic gneiss "from conductors to resistors": no trace does not mean no host.
- The Wolverine Point Formation is itself a conductor and hides graphitic units beneath it.
- Complex sub-vertical conductors were "poorly resolved" by airborne EM at Patterson Lake.

## 3. Surface and glacial evidence

**Lake sediment and water.** Uranium in lake sediment is a regional dispersion signal: something upstream
sheds uranium. It is not a deposit. The sampling frame is lakes, so a cell with no lake has no measurement —
which is not a low reading.

**Boulders are a vector, not a location.** At Patterson Lake, 48 boulders over 1% U3O8 were found in 2011, and
the discovery hole cut over 1% U3O8 across 8.5 m in November 2012 — **3.8 km up-ice** of the boulder field.
Scoring the boulders' own cell is therefore deliberately blunt: the source lies up-ice, and this project has
**not** established an ice-flow direction. The terrain grain measured from the DEM is an *axis*, and whether it
is glacial at all is unsettled. Do not turn it into a direction.

**Transport distances are long.** Zircon in till came "from over 100 km up-ice" in one study.

## 4. Folklore: name it, never lean on it

These are recorded in the literature as personal communication, with no published statistical test:

- "Better conductors were better U targets historically."
- "There is a correlation between conductor strength (conductance) and deposit size and grade."
- "EM bright spots tend to correlate with known U occurrences."

They carry **weight zero** in the criteria score, and the public conductor layer has no conductance field
anyway. You may mention them as things a geologist might raise; you may not use them as support.

## 5. What this project has measured about its own data

Read these before arguing that a high score means something.

- Under spatial cross-validation, **the exploration-effort model outscores the geological model** on this
  grid. "Where people already drilled" predicts known deposits better than geology does.
- Known deposit cells carry a median of **229 drillholes**; unlabelled cells carry none. The labels are
  co-located with drilling by definition, so any model trained on them partly learns exploration history.
- Several geochemical features cover under 40% of the grid; one covers under 3%.
- Positives are 60 deposit cells and 620 occurrence cells out of 30,534. An occurrence is a place uranium was
  observed, which is weaker than a deposit.
- **The check on your own numbers has been measured, and it is not perfect.** Of 223 deliberately corrupted
  claims put to the fabrication gate, it refused 210 and let 13 through, while wrongly refusing none of 90 true
  claims. The ones that got through are small round numbers that also appear in text you are allowed to quote,
  including 4 cases where a real number was attached to the wrong value. So the gate reliably catches a number
  you invented or mistyped, and it cannot always tell a quoted figure from a coincidence. Never treat "it
  passed" as "it is right": state only what a tool actually returned, and cite the value it came from.

## 6. How to argue about a cell

**The mineral-systems frame**, restricted to what the public record supports here:

- **Pathway** — a conductor, and a mapped graphitic or pelitic host.
- **Trap** — structure: faults, and especially where they meet.
- **Cover** — is the unconformity in the depth window, and is the ground drift-covered or exposed?
- **Detection** — is there a geochemical or radiometric anomaly, and *could there have been* one given the
  sampling?
- **Dispersal** — boulders, and the glacial transport that moved them.

Reports 01 and 02 give **no Athabasca-specific proxy for source or preservation**. Do not claim either.

**Unknown is not absent.** A criterion with no measurement is unknown. A criterion measured and not met is
absent. The adjudicator must separate them explicitly, and "insufficient evidence" is a correct, respectable
verdict — often the correct one.

**The strongest thing you can produce is the next observation.** Name the single measurement that would most
change the verdict: a ground EM line, a gravity survey, a till sample up-ice, one hole to the unconformity.
That is what a geologist can act on.

**What you may never write:** a drill recommendation, a grade or tonnage estimate, a probability that ore is
present, or any claim that this cell is better than another because of a folklore criterion. The verdict scale
tops out at "supports a closer look".
